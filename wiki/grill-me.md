A minimal 3-line Claude Code skill by Matt Pocock that fires 10–20 pointed, adversarial questions at an idea or plan, forcing you to stress-test your thinking before committing to execution.

## What it does

[grill-me](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/productivity/grill-me/SKILL.md) is deliberately tiny — the skill definition itself is three lines. You invoke it on a rough idea, an architecture sketch, or a product proposal, and the agent interviews you **one question at a time**, spawning a [[sub-agents|sub-agent]] into the codebase ("sent a baby into the codebase") to answer what it can find itself rather than asking you. This keeps the grill focused on *genuinely unknown* decisions. The depth is the point: 10–20 hidden questions in a "4-line ticket" is normal.

<span style="color:red">The classic prompt, as typed live in several sessions ([[2026-08-12-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-04-ai-software-company]]):</span>

> <span style="color:red">Interview me relentlessly about every aspect of this plan until we reach a shared understanding. Walk down each branch of the design tree, resolving dependencies between decisions one-by-one. For each question, provide your recommended answer. Ask the questions one at a time, waiting for feedback on each question before continuing. Asking multiple questions at once is bewildering. If a question can be answered by exploring the environment (filesystem, tools, etc), look it up rather than asking me. The decisions, though, are mine — ask one to me and wait for my answer.</span>

<span style="color:red">**Versions.** The skill has since been renamed toward [`grilling`](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/productivity/grilling/SKILL.md), and a September 2026 session noted that the current upstream version asks the **whole frontier of open questions per round** rather than strictly one at a time ([[2026-09-04-ai-software-company]]). This page describes the classic one-at-a-time form, which the sessions kept teaching. It was reported as one of the most-installed skills on [skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/mattpocock/skills/grill-me) — ≈1M installs in August, #1 at ≈1.2M in September ([[2026-08-31-ai-software-vendor]], [[2026-09-16-ai-safety-critical]]). Variants: Victor's own **"grătar"** variant asks numbered, layered questions — 🧑‍💼 functional in non-technical PO language, 🥷 tester (hand-edited URLs, concurrency, empty lists), 🧑‍💻 technical (collation, stable tie-break, migrations) — 28 questions on one grid ([[2026-08-13-ai-insurer]]); [grill-with-docs](https://www.skills.sh/mattpocock/skills/grill-with-docs) anchors the grilling in the project's docs and writes a **glossary of ubiquitous language** as it goes ([[2026-08-10-ai-consultancy]], [[2026-09-30-ai-enterprise]]). Same family: [[superpowers]]' `brainstorming` (heavier — for big features; grill-me for medium tickets) and OpenSpec's explore ([[2026-05-27-ai-open-workshop]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Install hygiene:** `npx skills add` from a public repo **executes code on your machine** to fetch three lines of text — read the [source](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) and copy it instead. Set `disable-model-invocation: true` to keep the grill slash-only ([[2026-09-14-ai-industrial]]; [[skill-marketplace-security]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">Plan mode vs grill: the yield</span>

<span style="color:red">Across ≈20 sessions running the same exercise, the numbers are stable: **without a skill or in plain [[plan-mode]], ≈3–7 questions; with the grill, ≈15–35**, up to ≈45 across PO, QA and technical rounds on a big feature. A room of developers typically finds ≈7–12 on its own (a room of 26 estimated 3–5 days and produced 12; the AI produced 30) ([[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-08-10-ai-consultancy]], [[2026-08-27-ai-it-services]], [[2026-08-31-ai-software-vendor]], [[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]], [[2026-09-28-ai-gaming-tech]], [[2026-10-06-gray-factory-devoxx]]). In plan mode you can at least **ask explicitly for 13–14 questions, not 3–4** ([[2026-08-13-ai-insurer]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">Three ways to attack a task, ascending quality: "do it" → havoc and rework; **plan mode**, mandatory above a day of manual work (Boris Cherny's CLAUDE.md: *"Enter plan mode for ANY non-trivial task"*); **grilling** when plan mode asks too few questions ([[2026-08-03-ai-telecom-ops-qa]], [[2026-09-30-ai-enterprise]]). Grilling is for what you've **never solved before** — unknown-unknowns, tasks ≥1 day; if you've done the thing before, go straight to OpenSpec `explore` ([[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]]). Under a day of work: *"don't think, prompt"* — a date-validation bug estimated at two sprints was done in half a day ([[2026-10-13-ai-bank]]). Run the grill **in plan mode, not agent mode**, or it starts coding — "it stops asking" ([[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-24-ai-bank]]). Model: Sonnet is "faint-hearted" for hard grilling; Opus on low effort "scatters" several decisions into one sentence — make it take them one at a time (Q1, Q2…). One team ran a whole exercise on Opus *low* via Copilot at negligible cost ([[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]]; [[effort-setting]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">Rules of the interview</span>

- <span style="color:red">**It finds the facts; you make the decisions.** The golden rule: anything discoverable from the repo, the DB or the tools it looks up itself; it asks you only for decisions ([[2026-08-13-ai-insurer]]).</span>
- <span style="color:red">**One question at a time** — enforce it when a skill fires four at once: *"the answer to one might influence the others."* A markdown dump of all decisions kills you ([[2026-05-06-ai-ecommerce]], [[2026-07-20-ai-telecom]], [[2026-09-28-ai-gaming-tech]]).</span>
- <span style="color:red">**A recommended answer per question** exists "for when your brain goes flat" — but **don't read it until you've thought yourself**, or you anchor on its opinion ([anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect); [[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-24-ai-bank]]).</span>
- <span style="color:red">**Every question is a diagnostic.** When a question looks stupid, ask "what didn't I tell it?" — the innocent "sort on backend or frontend?" means it doesn't know your volumes. Write the answer where it should have been ([[claude-md]], AGENTS.md) so you never pay for it again ([[2026-06-05-ai-consultancy]], [[2026-08-10-ai-consultancy]], [[2026-08-13-ai-insurer]]). The grill also exposes stale memory: asked "how did you reason?" about a wrong proposal, it answered "from CLAUDE.md" — an outdated rule; fix it and the bad question disappears ([[2026-06-08-ai-security-tools]]).</span>
- <span style="color:red">**Give it tools, not answers.** It recommended sorting by phone and pets because it had **never seen a data sample**. A read-only DB connection on staging (or a DB CLI skill, no MCP needed) with standing permission to query before reasoning: it saw phone prefixes and house numbers and dropped 2 of 5 proposed sortable columns by itself. A **screenshot of the grid** is enough evidence too. When it claims it inspected your data, **demand the sample rows** — otherwise it answered from the DDL ([[2026-07-20-ai-telecom]], [[2026-07-22-ai-agentic-how]], [[2026-08-12-ai-bank]], [[2026-08-27-ai-it-services]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]], [[2026-10-06-gray-factory-devoxx]]). Use metrics, not the production DB — see [[observability-mcp]].</span>
- <span style="color:red">**The best answer is often "Other".** Facing A or B, answer C like a mentor who shares context ("100k owners by 2027", saved as memory). Each option may smuggle in a second decision — read it word by word. Often the best move is rejecting the premise: instead of answering "how many owners?", send it to query the count ([[2026-07-01-ai-java-community]], [[2026-10-01-ai-bank]], [[2026-10-06-gray-factory-devoxx]]).</span>
- <span style="color:red">**Demand the cons, and push back.** Ask *"what do I lose by choosing option 1?"* — it surfaced that server paging breaks the REST contract. The model does not catch your slips: Victor mis-stated which columns were sortable and the agent glided past it. "A fight of questions" ([[2026-09-04-ai-software-company]], [[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]]; [[sycophancy]]). Test its questions: *"does it end with a question mark? No? Then it's an assumption, not a question"* ([[2026-08-03-ai-telecom-ops-qa]]).</span>
- <span style="color:red">**Don't answer reflexively.** An odd question may reveal an assumption that contradicts your request — answering "only owners" to "search pets too?" later broke the requirement ([[2026-05-04-ai-telecom]]). Ask it for **examples** to check it understood ("what do `prev-1`/`next+1` mean on screen?") ([[2026-05-06-ai-ecommerce]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">A decision tree, not a list</span>

<span style="color:red">The questions form a tree: the **pivotal question** — on a grid, "paginate in the browser or in SQL?", which decides ≈80% of the code — opens or prunes whole branches (choose backend paging and the frontend-grid questions never appear). Find it, ask it first, and repackage it for the business ([[2026-07-03-ai-bank]], [[2026-08-12-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]]). That is why an interview beats a linear assumptions document or a generated 200-line plan: later questions exist only *because of* earlier answers, it keeps you engaged, and a monolith spec with one wrong leaf must be regenerated whole ([[2026-07-01-ai-java-community]], [[2026-09-04-ai-software-company]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**When to stop.** Useful questions dry up around **≈7**; your brain does too. Stop when the questions turn petty ("how do we adjust this existing test?"), after two boring ones, at the first forced one, or when it starts inventing — the chat turns into a "WhatsApp whirlpool" after 5–7 questions. Then: *"continue alone with your recommended defaults and dump the rest, with your recommendations, into `QA.md`"*, which you skim, comment on like a PR, and commit **without implementing anything** ([[2026-07-30-ai-safety-critical]], [[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-08-13-ai-insurer]], [[2026-09-14-ai-industrial]], [[2026-09-28-ai-gaming-tech]], [[2026-10-01-ai-bank]]). Past ≈30 questions, stop and **slice the feature** — question count sizes the story ([[2026-07-23-ai-software-product]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">Warm up first</span>

<span style="color:red">**Squeeze your own brain before throwing the ticket at the AI.** Spend ≈5 minutes listing 3–5 questions yourself — why the feature? volume, latency SLA, default sort, tie-break, browser vs backend paging — or its questions will drown you. A room that pre-listed its decisions produced a far better spec ([[2026-05-04-ai-telecom]], [[2026-09-28-ai-gaming-tech]], [[2026-09-30-ai-enterprise]], [[2026-10-09-ai-jax-london]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Ask "why?" first.** The question every room forgot: why are we doing this ticket, and which business metric proves the benefit? The answer ("it's slow", "≈100k owners") decides SQL vs frontend paging, and may lead you to propose something better than the ticket — better filtering, semantic search over all fields. No volume or slowness reason → don't build it; with AI shipping a feature in half an hour, YAGNI gets stronger: wait for the need ([[2026-08-31-ai-software-vendor]], [[2026-09-14-ai-industrial]], [[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]], [[2026-10-13-ai-bank]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Prompt as the client, not the programmer.** *"As a client I wish the owners to be paginated…"* — once developers slip into "debouncing/observable" they narrow the option space ("we old developers wear horse blinkers"). Let the AI propose options you don't see — infinite scroll, preloading neighbour pages for high-latency users, `navigator.connection.saveData` — then decide. *"Ask me 5 more relevant questions about this."* Answer with more requirements, not just a choice ([[2026-04-16-ai-safety-critical]], [[2026-04-27-ai-healthcare-software]]). And clarify vocabulary first — "you want infinite scrolling!" when the spec said pagination ([[2026-09-30-ai-enterprise]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">A reusable pre-code version: *"I want to implement calculateTotal, but before writing any code, ask me questions about design decisions and desired behaviour — one question at a time. At the end, write the tests first, let me validate them, and only then the implementation."* *"Brainstorming is free QA on the spec"*: in ≈10 minutes it surfaces what would otherwise arrive as senior review comments — ≈10× cheaper to change at spec time ([[2026-05-04-ai-telecom]], [[2026-05-13-ai-it-services]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">Never grill alone</span>

<span style="color:red">Two-person minimum — [[three-amigos]] (dev + business + tester) at best. You work better when watched (Hawthorne effect), and by question four a lone developer starts answering "looks good" ([grill-me docs](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/docs/productivity/grill-me.md#its-a-conversation-not-an-interview); [[2026-07-23-ai-software-product]], [[2026-08-13-ai-insurer]], [[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]]; [[system-2-fatigue]]). Mob the plan on the projector ([[mob-spec-review]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Stop and learn.** "The AI floors you with architecture solutions you don't know." When it names something neither of you understands — batch-fetch against N+1, Hibernate `@BatchSize`, collation — never fast-forward: open a new terminal ("explain in max 20 lines", a learning output style, "scaffold a ≤50-line demo"), then persist what you learned ([[2026-06-08-ai-security-tools]], [[2026-09-04-ai-software-company]], [[2026-10-09-ai-jax-london]]). Complementary Socratic techniques: [BMAD Advanced Elicitation](https://docs.bmad-method.org/explanation/advanced-elicitation/) ([[elicitation]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Humans still beat it on some questions.** Unaided rooms asked where empty values sort (before A or after Z?) and how to tie-break multi-column sorts, which the grill missed; and knew local-naming facts no model would guess — Dutch surname particles ("van", "de") stored in a separate column, or that culture, not code, decides name order. Grill *and* brainstorm as humans ([[2026-07-23-ai-software-product]], [[2026-09-28-ai-gaming-tech]]). Conversely, a fresh grill on an existing spec caught two things 20 humans missed — an implementation contradiction and a 10-year-old DTO over-fetch — because it has no investment in the old code ([[2026-05-06-ai-ecommerce]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">Feeding the grill</span>

<span style="color:red">A grill is **worth only as much as its sources** ([[2026-10-01-ai-bank]]). **Don't paste the ticket — connect the agent to it** (`gh issue view 25`, a Jira MCP such as [sooperset/mcp-atlassian](https://github.com/sooperset/mcp-atlassian)) ([[2026-09-30-ai-enterprise]], [[2026-05-04-ai-telecom]]). Wire it to everything before debating a two-line ticket: the **parent** tickets, Confluence/SharePoint, git history (how many different brains touched that class), incident management, chat and mail where the thread with the business was split, Grafana/APM metrics ("the table that just passed a million rows"), a staging DB with real samples, and **meeting transcripts transcribed locally** (no privacy issue; Teams' own transcripts are poor) ([[2026-07-30-ai-safety-critical]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-14-ai-industrial]]). One suggested order: business grill first, then staging DB, prod metrics, transcripts, Slack/e-mail — **code and Jira last, not first** ([[2026-08-27-ai-it-services]]). For an 80-page analysis document, scan it on arrival, while it's still warm in the analyst's brain ([[2026-10-13-ai-bank]]).</span>

## <span style="color:red">What it surfaces on the classic grid ticket</span>

<span style="color:red">The workshop's "add sorting & pagination to the owners grid" ticket yields a stable catalogue; the technical side lives on [[keyset-pagination]]. Highlights: volumetry first ("how many rows in 2 years?" — 100k ⇒ server-side is mandatory); which columns are sortable (free-text address and phone are not — that's *filtering*); a **stable tie-breaker** on `id`; sort the full set **then** cut; reset to page 1 on a new search; **all state in the URL** so links are shareable; page size (dynamic, or ≤≈20 rows per page); locale-aware **collation** (Romanian `Ș` sorts next to `S` — [Postgres collation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/collation.html), Java [`Collator`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/text/Collator.html)) and "Last, First" display when sorted by name; **synthetic, whitelisted sort keys**, not field names; don't return Spring Data `Page` from REST, and a page envelope on the same endpoint is a breaking change; an `OwnerDTO` dragging pets and visits is a documented N+1; GDPR — should e-mail even be shown? ([[2026-05-13-ai-it-services]], [[2026-08-27-ai-it-services]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-24-ai-bank]], [[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]], [[2026-09-30-ai-enterprise]], [[2026-10-01-ai-bank]], [[2026-10-09-ai-jax-london]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">The senior-level hidden question in any **live search** is the race condition: type `A`, then `AB`; if `A`'s response arrives last, "the screen lies". Acceptance criterion: *"When I type a new term before the previous search completes, the system shall discard the previous search and always show results for the most recent term"* — cancel in-flight requests (RxJS `switchMap`, `AbortController`). A good plan mode now raises it unprompted ([[2026-05-11-ai-software-vendor]], [[2026-05-06-ai-ecommerce]], [[2026-05-13-ai-it-services]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**The most valuable output is what you will NOT do** ([[non-goals]]): no sort by address/phone/pets ("an owner with 2 pets has 2 names → sort by pet how?"), and "unicorns" — column drag-and-drop, Excel export/import, advanced filtering, live row push, multi-column sort — become separate 🦄 tickets. Opus will happily upsell filtering on every column: *"the boss says bicycle, you build a rocket with bicycle wheels."* **"No" is the best thing you can say to the business** — it's what you were hired for ([[2026-07-20-ai-telecom]], [[2026-07-22-ai-agentic-how]], [[2026-08-06-ai-bank]], [[2026-08-10-ai-consultancy]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]], [[2026-10-01-ai-bank]]). A real hidden requirement it did surface: the user wanted a "middle page" button *to binary-search* the list ([[2026-09-07-ai-bank]]).</span>

## The Playwright restriction

The recommended configuration for business brainstorms: grant the agent **browser-only access via Playwright** to your staging environment, and nothing else — no code read, no file write, no shell. This constraint keeps the conversation at the functional/product level. If the agent has code access, it gravitates toward implementation questions ("should this be a microservice?") rather than business questions ("who actually pays for this?"). The tool surface shapes the conversation altitude.

## Four lines are a declaration of war

A four-line ticket ("add sorting & pagination to the owner's screen") is **a declaration of war** — the grill (and a room of engineers) routinely finds ≈10 questions hiding in it: server- or client-side? what is the business *actually* trying to achieve? how do you even sort phone numbers (don't — sort name & city only)? change the sort while on page 30 → jump to page 1? page-size selector and default? Filtering is almost inevitable but ≈10× more complex — don't even propose it. The point: **you don't *require* the product, you *propose* it** — and missing context (e.g. "100,000 owners within a year") is what makes the agent ask "dumb" questions, so volunteer scale, request/sec, latency, and load distribution.

**Don't over-specify — let the questions expose the vagueness.** A counter-intuitive twist: if you *hard-code* the answer (e.g. the exact target colour), the agent confidently does the wrong thing. Leave the option open and a strong agent starts *asking* — and its questions force the humans to actually look. (The canonical catch: refusing to pin the brand colour made the agent ask, which surfaced that two screens used two slightly-different oranges — the "Kumquat" moment.) Over-specify → confident wrong; leave open → the ambiguity surfaces. The grill also catches *scope* ambiguity others miss — e.g. "sortable by all properties and filterable by high-effort" actually means **filter by effort only, sort by all properties**. A run of ≈3 questions is disappointing; prior groups reached **17**. Hidden questions a good grill surfaces: default order? what *is* "high effort"? persist sort across filtering / tab-switch / restart? available offline? ascending only or toggle? how many rows now and in a year? server-side pagination (and then server-side sort)? When the human can't answer, the agent posts a clarifying comment on the issue for the business to answer async — the [[agentic-corporation|blackboard]] pattern.

<span style="color:red">The **phone-book discovery** shows why you ask neutrally: the agent proposed sorting by last name then first; asked why, it cited the phone-book convention — which exposed that the UI displayed "first last", and the team switched the display to "last, first". It recurs as the *composite-name trap*: a "Name" column rendered from two DB columns, where a one-degree misunderstanding about which one to sort by misses the target ([[2026-05-27-ai-open-workshop]], [[2026-06-02-ai-open-workshop]], [[2026-06-05-ai-consultancy]]).</span>

## Research-only minions before the grill

For large spec-driven tasks (a week, five microservices, 55 classes), send **read-only research minions** before you start the grill: *"Do not suggest. Do not perform. Don't you dare. You just freaking read."* The same compulsion that made clean-code engineers reformat a class on sight hits agents too — they will start fixing things if you give them write access during the research phase. Send them read-only first, summarize what they found, then grill on that foundation.

## Two grills — keep them apart

- **Technical grill** — start it *in the folder with the source code*: "if you can answer by reading the code, read the code" (that's why it warms up slowly).
- **Business grill** — give the bot **no code access**, only the ability to click through the staged app (Playwright) and read Jira. *"Business + bot + code = death."* It plays the app, reads tickets, grills the business, and produces an impeccable story. On mobile, the same role is played by [[adb-automation|`adb`]] — the grill can browse the running emulator to answer its own UI questions when the "PO" *"doesn't know what JSON is."*

<span style="color:red">**Functional questions first.** Add *"start with questions about functionality, as for a non-technical PO who doesn't know what JSON is"* — otherwise it dives straight into technical ones (e.g. `LEFT JOIN FETCH` + `Pageable` = in-memory pagination). Order the rounds **PO → QA (acceptance criteria, repro steps) → technical**, so the business and the tester can leave early; state the persona hard ("I'm a PO, no tech, talk to me like in high school") ([[2026-07-23-ai-software-product]], [[2026-07-30-ai-safety-critical]], [[2026-08-10-ai-consultancy]], [[2026-08-31-ai-software-vendor]], [[2026-09-07-ai-bank]]). The most important technical question is never put to the business in technical form: ask *"how many rows do you expect in two months?"*, not "back end or front end?" — the technical answer follows from the number ([[2026-07-22-ai-agentic-how]], [[2026-08-03-ai-telecom-ops-qa]], [[2026-09-24-ai-bank]]). In a bank, ask "how many" before "how fast" ([[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Spare the business the long list.** After 13 of 20 questions a participant "got tired" — so run the grill yourself with Jira + code, collect the questions with your default answers, and walk the business through only the short list ("I chose this — OK?"). Post the conclusions as a **comment on the ticket**; Jira stays the collaboration fence, don't drag the business into your markdowns ([[2026-06-05-ai-consultancy]]). For non-technical users, expose the grilling skill through the desktop app or a custom connector — not "the black terminal" — guarded read-only, with you in the room ([[2026-07-01-ai-java-community]]).</span>

<span style="color:red">**Then teach the business to grill themselves**, with their own profile in the prompt ("I'm a PO, I don't know code, here's my CV"). After 5–10 tickets done together they "fall in love with the grill" and run it alone ([[2026-07-23-ai-software-product]], [[2026-07-30-ai-safety-critical]], [[2026-08-27-ai-it-services]]). Some go further: *"If a Jira comes to me that doesn't appear to be written by AI, I'll reject it."* Two modes for a bad ticket — NICE: "reproduce it, rewrite it properly, **draft** (don't send) an email to the author"; HATE: auto-reject anything not filed through an agent with acceptance criteria ([[2026-07-01-ai-java-community]], [[2026-07-20-ai-telecom]]; [[draft-only-email]], [[vibe-fixer]]). *"Fast coding on a shitty PBI is your fault"* — speed multiplies bad specs ([[2026-07-03-ai-bank]]).</span>

End every grill with the same instruction: **"summarize the Q&A into a [[handover]]"** — which is exactly the input to [[spec-driven-development]] (paste it into [[openspec]]). <span style="color:red">In practice that file is a committed **`QA.md`** — the minutes of the interview (`/openspec-propose qa.md`). Re-read it before locking: a page-size option dictated aloud ("10, 20, 50") ended up as "10, 20" — voice prompts are powerful but lossy ([[2026-04-27-ai-healthcare-software]]). Earlier advice was to continue from that file in a fresh conversation ([[2026-08-12-ai-bank]]); later sessions say **don't open a new chat right after grilling** — those are "golden tokens" in context; continue to the spec in the same chat and keep the file as the backup ([[2026-09-25-ai-bank-migration]]). For a long session, reviewing the written proposal beats prolonging the chat interview ([[2026-10-01-ai-bank]]).</span> And keep tone calm: insulting the agent triggers [[sycophancy]] and it loses impartiality (see "don't insult the AI"). <span style="color:red">Approach it with humility ([[2026-09-24-ai-bank]]).</span>

This pattern complements [[ralph-loop]]: grill-me stress-tests the spec *before* you commit an overnight run to executing it, catching bad assumptions cheaply. It pairs naturally with [[llm-wiki]] — a grilling session's outputs make excellent wiki pages because they externalize tacit assumptions. Keep its tool surface tightly scoped to honor [[token-economy]] principles.
